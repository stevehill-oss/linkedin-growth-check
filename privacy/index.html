Privacy and account access

Two things people reasonably want to know before they get in touch: what happens to what they send, and how I work on their LinkedIn without their password.

How your information is handled

This covers the enquiry form on the main page and the LinkedIn check.

Who is responsible for it

,

What I collect

Only what you choose to send. From the enquiry form: your name, work email, company, role and any message. From the LinkedIn check: your answers, your score, the quick details you pick and the contact details you type in. Nothing is sent until you press the button.

Why, and the legal basis

To reply to your enquiry or your request for a free review. The legal basis is that you’ve asked me to do something before we agree any contract.

Who else handles it

The forms are delivered to my inbox by Web3Forms, an email form service. Its privacy policy (opens in a new tab) says it operates from India, keeps form submissions for up to three years and uses standard contractual clauses for transfers from the UK. It also says your IP address and email address may be sent to two spam-checking services, CleanTalk and Akismet.

If you send from your own email app, your message goes through your own email provider.

How long I keep it

Only as long as I need it to deal with your request. I’ll delete it sooner if you ask.

What I don’t do

I never sell what you send, and I don’t add you to any mailing list unless you say yes.

Your rights

You can ask to see, correct or delete what I hold about you, or object to how I use it. If you’re unhappy with how I handle it, you can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office (ico.org.uk (opens in a new tab)).

Cookies and storage

These pages set no cookies and run no tracking or analytics. While you take the check, your answers are kept in your browser tab only, so a reload doesn’t lose them. Closing the tab clears them.

How account access works

Your account stays in your hands, and I never ask for your password.

I never ask for your password

For your personal profile, every post is a draft you approve first. Then you post it, or it goes out through a scheduling tool you connect to your own account. For a company page, you add me through LinkedIn’s own admin settings.

Nothing goes out without your yes

You see every post before it’s published. Edit it, send it back or approve it.

Everything stays yours

Your profile, your page, your posts and your connections belong to you. If we stop working together, you keep the lot and my access ends.

You can remove me at any time

Company page admins and connected tools are both switched off from LinkedIn’s own settings, whenever you like.

Your personal profile

  1. I write each post as a draft for you to approve.
  2. You post it yourself, or a scheduling tool posts it after you’ve signed in to LinkedIn and allowed it.
  3. You can switch off any connected tool in LinkedIn’s settings, under Data privacy, whenever you like.

Your company page (if you add company page support)

  1. You stay super admin, the top role, so you always control who has access.
  2. You add me as an admin from your page’s settings, under Manage admins. LinkedIn asks that I follow your page first.
  3. You can remove me from that same screen at any time.

These pages set no tracking cookies, so nothing follows you around the web afterwards.
